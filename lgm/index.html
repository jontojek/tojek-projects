Jon Tojek has been at Alias|Wavefront for the past 6 months working as an Application Engineer whose responsibilities include demoing and training people in all of the Toronto based companies 2D and 3D software. Visiting customers that range from ILM, PDI, and Rhythm and Hues doing high-end flim and video for the masses down to hungry start ups working in commercials and games such as Radium, Little Fluffy Clouds, and H-gun. Before this he was heading up the 3D department for a Full-service special effects house in Chicago called Post Effects.

This was a 60+ large scale post facility with a long history of effects and happened to be the first north american Alias entertainment customer. Here his first responsiblility was to build a team of animators and helm a department that included 7 SGI workstations inside a 9 bay environment. "It was at Post Effects that I learned the business of client relations, making quotes, management, and sacrificing myself on the alter of hard-core work," Tojek commented.

For two years before that he was once again in the corporate fold of Alias chicago doing the AE job and considers that to be the place where he did his most lasting work such as DevilBoy:Documentary on Clowns, and The UrbanWorm. Other stints include more animator work at CyberToons in Milwaukee, Electrical Engineering graduate from University of Wisconsin Madison, and relentlessly part time but obsessed electronic musician who scores all of his animations and videos. Currently his latest project is a 35mm short entitled "Let's get medical" which deals with his current interest in medical equipment and practices.

JOEL MARIANO

Born in the Philippines, Joel Mariano is one generation removed from the rice farm. He began living in the USA at the age of 6. Athough he grew up here, his childhood memories of the tropics have had an everlasting influence on his art in terms of movement, form, and color. As an undergraduate, he studied classical art in Europe--concentrating on Classical Greek Sculpture and the Italian High Renaissance. Double majoring in Fine Art and Journalism for his undergraduate studies, he was able to experience a firm foundation in drawing, painting, printmaking, and clay figurative sculpture.

His first exposure to seriously using computers to create art was at the California Institute of the Arts--where he got his Master of Fine Arts degree specializing in Experimental Animation. At CalArts from 1984-87, he played with real-time analog video graphics, and began exploring 3D animation systems. Tired of getting limited cubicomp time, Joel used his scholarship money to purchase an Amiga computer--this move changed his life.

Since then, he has realized that computer graphic imaging was a medium that allowed him to combine all his aesthetic interests. For the past 10 years, his focus has been on new ways of achieving aesthetic results that could only be made from this medium. His goal in his abstracts is to achieve harmonious compositions from a pure epiphany of colors and tones--devoid of ego--but spiritually fulfilling.

SHANE COOPER

Shane Cooper studied computer enginering at the University of California San Diego, durring which time he worked at the San Diego Super Computer Center on numerous graphics intensive simulations and independent projects. Around then, he was also working in the science of particle physics at UCSD and the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center. He later moved to San Francisco, where he worked with George Coates Performance Works as a computer effects designer, creating abstract graphics applications to interact in realtime with actors live, on stage, and in 3D.

These works were showcased in the prestigious Spaleto Festival in South Carolina as well performed in Tokyo as part of the Intercommunications project funded by NTT. In San Francisco, he also worked on various projects for Silicon Graphics including some graphic effects work for motion pictures, and realtime visual effects for the Macy's Passport Fashion Show. He worked in Los Angeles with Silicon Studio LA developing live fully interactive realtime graphics performance software.

This is being used successfully by Graham Nash in his Lifesighs show, which he has recently performed in Atlanta. Shane Cooper currently works for Protozoa in San Francisco, developing realtime performance software for performing interactive virtual characters and sets for television broadcast and the web (see In his spare time, he creates more realtime abstract computer works for the SGI and PC platforms. He has many other interests that are less visual, and they include computer generated text, computer music, artificial intelligence, voluntary human control via computer, animal controled art, and the usage of real ant systems for solving problems.

THE WORK BEHIND THE WORK

How this site was made

This site was assembled by Hermes Agent as a local, searchable way to turn a difficult private archive into a readable production history.

The original tar files were located, and three raw archive streams—compressed and encrypted at source—were searched and indexed without sending the archive to a remote service. Headers, dates, correspondents, and relationships were organized first; selected story material could then be assembled from the first email to the last in a clear chronological way.

That means the LGM story is not an isolated web page. It is one small, carefully selected part of a much larger main archive: 1,081 emails involving 1,367 indexed people and contact nodes across five calendar years, 1995–1999. The same structure can now support many different stories, each with its own beginning, middle, end, evidence, and visual record.

Everything here was created locally with Hermes and AI sovereignty: local files, local indexing, local presentation, and human direction at every meaningful step.

Jon Tojek does not claim to know anything about web design. He does, however, yell verbally at the computer—and at Hermes—until the page looks good. That is part of the production process too.
