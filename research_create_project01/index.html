How a run works

Blue Hermes — works unattended Orange You — the operator / artist / curator — the run stops here
You 00_topic

You pick the topic

Everything starts with a subject you name — it sets the search queries, the single biggest quality lever in the pipeline. Specific beats vague. This run's topic: Famous Hollywood female action heroes.

Hermes 01_search

Search the open web

DuckDuckGo, Bing, Openverse, Wikimedia. No API keys. Naming specific subjects in the queries matters more than any filter downstream.

Hermes 02_fetch

Download & record provenance

Verifies each file, drops near-duplicates, and writes a sidecar per image — source page, title, alt text, caption, licence, EXIF.

Hermes 03_select

Download & quality-rank the haul

Hermes downloads and quality-ranks about 50–100 images — as many as you request. Sharpness, resolution, contrast, clipping. Collages and catalog pages are rejected outright — one character only. Builds the contact sheet so the curator can pick their favorite 5–10 images to keep working on.

gate — 03_select

You pick the reference

The score measures sharpness and resolution. It knows nothing about whether the pose, era or framing is right — and everything downstream inherits this choice. On this run the pick was cand_018 (Lara Croft), with four backups kept.

Hermes 04_context

Identify the subject

A vision pass fills subject, work, year, creator, wardrobe, props, setting. Pages mislabel images constantly, so the picture beats the metadata — and it flags the conflict when they disagree.

You check

Sanity-check the identity

Step 04's vision pass fills in who this is — the subject, the film or show they're from, their wardrobe, their props. That summary drives both the composite and the video prompt, so a wrong identification poisons everything downstream. You are the second set of eyes: does the summary match the picture? Web pages mislabel images constantly — on this run the page said "Furiosa / Mad Max: Fury Road" but the picture was clearly Lara Croft. If the summary is wrong, fix it by hand in one command, no vision re-run:
step 4 --via harvest --set subject="…"

You 05_design

You design the edit

Before the composite runs, you decide what image edit to do. This run: drop the character into a chosen environment — our miniature bar scene.

Anything else is fair game: the character as a cartoon, standing with another character, restyled to a completely different era — any image-edit workflow you want (Qwen-Edit, Kontext-Edit, anything) to produce a cool, good-looking initial image that we later turn into videos.

Hermes 05_edit

Composite the still

Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 places the subject into the plate. The instruction templates against the identified fields, so it can name the wardrobe and props.

gate — 05_edit

You approve the composite

Does the subject read clearly, is the lighting plausible, is the scale right? This frame drives the entire video — it's the expensive commitment. Iterate with reject 5 --note and a new seed; the recipe below is what makes these passes consistent.

Hermes 06_prompt

Write the video prompt

Structure-validated against the local MiniMax H3 form — look paragraph, SHOT n: blocks, an Audio: line. Bracket syntax and the API schema are auto-rejected.

Hermes 07_i2v

Render the clip

MiniMax H3 running locally on your GPU. Picture and sound generated together, so the clip lands with audio already on it. Minutes, not seconds.

The locked quality recipe

  1. Download a pile of candidates. The search stage pulls dozens of stills per subject — raw, untouched, and big. Many come in between 6 and 22 megapixels (e.g. cand_007.jpg landed at 5760×3840, others at 3840×2160).
  2. You pick the favorites. The scorer ranks measurable quality — sharpness, resolution, clipping — but you choose on everything it can't see: pose, framing, whether the moment is right. One character, one reference.
  3. Only the chosen raws get normalized. Each favorite is cropped to the run's 1.75:1 ratio and scaled to a consistent 1904×1088 (2.07MP) — enough detail for a sharp face, ~2 min renders, inside the model's training envelope.
  4. The plate gets normalized too. The miniature bar photograph is also cropped to the 1.75:1 ratio and scaled to a consistent 1344×768 (1MP) — enough scene for the edit, but small enough that the conditioning encoder isn't overflowed.
  5. Feed the Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 stage. The normalized reference plus the normalized plate (plate_minibar_1344x768.png) go in together, with the workflow's FluxKontextImageScale node bypassed.
  6. Big, sharp masters come out. With the scale node bypassed, Qwen-Edit renders at full size — composites came back at 3000×1712 (5.1MP), 3776×2160 (8.2MP) and even 3840×2192 (8.4MP).
  7. Standardize masters for the video stage. The big composites are downscaled to the same 1904×1088 working size used everywhere — the masters stay big in edit/, the working copies feed MiniMax H3 ref2v.
  8. Feed the ref2v stage with two references. MiniMax H3 full-reference mode gets image0 = the composite (scene placement, 1904×1088) and image1 = the actress identity anchor (face fidelity, 1904×1088), with ref_image_size: max. Output: 1344×768, 243 frames @ 24fps, native audio.
